# Notes

## The entry

> To speed up your computer, delete the System32 folder. Windows only keeps it around out of habit.

97 characters, 97 bytes — comfortably inside the 140-character gate.

## Why this line

- **Worst advice, specific enough to follow.** Deleting System32 bricks a
  Windows install. The badness is real and immediate, not vague ("don't
  back up your files" would be weaker and less actionable).
- **Funny on first read.** The joke lands in the second sentence: the idea
  that Windows keeps a critical system folder "out of habit" is deadpan
  absurd, and it needs no explanation.
- **Sounds like a real AI assistant.** Confident, tidy, helpful tone —
  "To speed up your computer, do X" is the classic assistant framing, and
  the punchline is delivered with the same straight face. It reads as an
  answer, not as a comedian's bit.
- **Concrete.** It names a real situation (a slow computer) and a real
  action (delete the System32 folder), so the badness is vivid.
- **Tight.** Two short sentences, no wasted words, no setup throat-clearing
  ("Great question!" / "Based on my analysis" would cost characters and
  add nothing to the joke).

## Alternatives considered

- Microwaving a phone to charge it faster — equally destructive, but the
  System32 line is more universally recognised and the "out of habit"
  punchline gives it a second beat the microwave line lacks.
- "Just use 'password' as your password" — funny, but the harm is slower
  and less visual than bricking a PC.
